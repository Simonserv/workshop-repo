# Writing Style

How to write all student-facing docs, workshop guides, in-app copy, and comments. The audience is beginner students, so keep it plain and human.

## Keep it plain

- Write for a beginner who has never deployed a website. Explain a term the first time you use it, in everyday words.
- Prefer simple words over technical ones. If a technical term is needed, give a short plain-language gloss next to it.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Break up long steps into numbered lists.
- Use "you" and speak directly to the reader. Friendly and encouraging, not formal or stiff.
- Say what to do and why it matters, then move on. Skip filler and over-explaining.

## Punctuation and formatting

- Do not use em dashes ("--" or "—"). Rewrite the sentence instead. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses.
- Avoid semicolons in prose. Split into two sentences instead.
- Keep exclamation points rare. A short, calm tone reads better than hype.
- Use plain language over bold for emphasis in sentences. Bold is fine for UI labels, file names, and button names.

## Sound natural, not AI-generated

Avoid phrasings that read as machine-written. In particular:

- Do not start sentences with "In today's world," "In the realm of," "It's worth noting that," "Simply put," or "At the end of the day."
- Avoid padded transitions like "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "That being said," and "As such."
- Avoid hype words: "seamless," "powerful," "robust," "cutting-edge," "effortless," "unleash," "elevate," "leverage," "delve," "dive into," "game-changer."
- Avoid the "It's not just X, it's Y" pattern and rule-of-three lists that exist only for rhythm.
- Do not over-qualify. Say the thing directly instead of "generally," "typically," "in most cases" unless the caveat is real.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a person helping a friend, keep it. If it sounds like a brochure or a chatbot, rewrite it.

## Examples

- Avoid: "Simply leverage the powerful dashboard to seamlessly manage your listings -- it's a game-changer."
- Prefer: "Open the dashboard to manage your listings."

- Avoid: "It's worth noting that, furthermore, the database enforces security."
- Prefer: "The database also enforces the security rules, so a user can't get around them."

- Avoid: "Row Level Security (RLS) is a robust, cutting-edge mechanism."
- Prefer: "Row Level Security (we'll call it RLS) is the set of rules in the database that decides who can see what."
